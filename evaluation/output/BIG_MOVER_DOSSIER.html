Big-Mover Momentum — Project Dossier

Everything we've done, tried, kept and dropped — in one read. Built from the winners databank (1,529 moves, 949 tickers). Two layers: (1) a signal — the rules + the TradingView indicator; (2) a field guide — how setups actually behave, so when a live chart deviates from the playbook you can judge it yourself. The sidebar holds the contents and a glossary that stays in view as you scroll. Last updated this session.

★ Where we are now

One honest sentence: we have a validated momentum framework whose entry signal has no standalone edge (in the wild it's ~breakeven) — it makes money as a risk-management + right-tail machine: cap every loser at ~1R, hold the rare monster, and (optionally) ride the later legs. The rules survive a 50/50 train/test split and a 6,571-signal control group.
The locked strategy: momentum universe (ADR 4–8%, liquid) · enter early & not extended · structural swing-low stop · 3-bar shakeout hold · then either Mode B (free roll — trim ½ at +2R, steadier, default) or Mode A (hold to the 50-MA — max R). An optional continuation toggle adds leg-2/leg-3 entries. Shipped as a clean-compiling TradingView indicator.

Expect live: ~30% win rate (not the database's 50–60%), small positive per-trade expectancy carried by the right tail. Size so a −1R loss ≈ 0.5–1% of equity.

The goal

Build a momentum strategy from the big-winner databank that maximises return (not win rate; target a high-20s–40% win band), with a defined entry, a portfolio-risk-based stop (1R), and a trail rule — possibly regime-dependent. Critically: robust, not overfit — usable in real trading, not a hindsight curve-fit. Take care of every part (entry, stop, trail), because the same setup traded with a different stop and trail is a different strategy.

The journey — what we tried, found, and decided

Seven phases, in order. Each: what we tried, what we found, the verdict.

1Myth-testing studydone

TriedValidated textbook momentum principles (Qullamaggie / Minervini / O'Neil) across all 1,529 winner moves — 24,580 point-in-time events, each anchored to a deterministic event + fixed window (the fix for the old 'fuzzy setup boundary' problem).
FoundYour holdability gates held up (winners spend ~82% of days above the 10-EMA vs 64% for laggards; ~3 vs ~15 touches of the 50-SMA). But many famous rules busted on this data: volume-surge-as-prerequisite (only ~26% of winner breakouts were ≥2× volume), the Minervini trend template / 52-week-high / RS as return edges, and the SPY>50SMA tape filter.
VerdictMost 'rules' are descriptions of winners, not predictors. Kept the holdability gates; discarded the rest as return edges. myth_report.html

2Strategy v1 — the policy gridsuperseded

TriedSimulated 396,732 trades: 4 stops × 7 exits × 14,185 deduped entries, gap-aware fills, no lookahead. Searched for the best entry/stop/exit on the ADR 4–8% momentum universe.

VerdictGood relative rankings, but every absolute number was in-sample and winners-only — an optimistic ceiling. Superseded by the overfit-proofing in Phase 6. strategy_playbook.html

6Overfit-proofing — train/test + control groupturning point

TriedThe big methodological reset. (a) Split the 949 tickers 50/50 by symbol; select on train, read the held-out test once. (b) Built a CONTROL group — the same signals firing on charts that did NOT become recorded movers (6,571 signals) — to expose survivorship.
FoundThe old 4-filter stack carried a −36% overfit tax (two of its four filters were noise that flip sign out-of-sample). Only TWO filters generalise — enter early and not extended, both about entry timing. And the punchline: the entry signal has ≈0 standalone edge — in the wild the average trade is roughly breakeven. The winners-set profit is survivorship.
VerdictReframed the whole thing: this is a risk-management + right-tail machine, not a high-edge signal. The honest, locked strategy comes from here. honest_findings.html

7Continuation (leg 2+) entriesdone

TriedYou flagged that the base-only entry misses the bulk of strong trends. Tested a continuation entry — a pullback that reclaims the 20-EMA inside an uptrend — at every extension band, on train / test / control.
FoundContinuation entries stay positive out-of-sample at every extension (~1.0–1.3R), and their control behaviour is slightly positive — better than the base breakout. 90.5% of winning moves offered one; allowing them roughly triples the R captured per move and re-enters after a shakeout.
VerdictWorth it. Shipped as an OPTIONAL, off-by-default toggle in the indicator — your call whether to run it. continuation_results.json

What actually moves the odds (and what doesn't):
• Depth — weak. Hit-rate is ~flat 46–50% across depths; shallower bases give somewhat higher forward returns. Don't skip a 30–40% base.
• Tightness — not a success edge. Looser bases hit +2R as often as very tight ones. Tightness matters for your stop size and R, not for whether the breakout works. The "must be tight" rule is overrated as a predictor.
• Contraction — the one that matters, and it's counterintuitive. Bases with expanding recent range (energy building, 1.2+) hit +2R 54% vs dead-quiet contracted bases (<0.5) at 42% — the opposite of "wait for it to go silent." Constructive action into the breakout beats a dead base (sharper still early in a move: 59% vs 39%).
How to use it. When a setup deviates from the textbook, these tell you whether to worry: a slightly deep or loose base is not a reason to pass; a base that's gone completely dead (heavy contraction, no energy) is the genuine yellow flag. Winners-only data, so trust the direction of each effect, not the absolute hit rates.

What works · what doesn't

✓ What works (kept)

  • Enter early + not extended (for base 1) — The two filters that survive out-of-sample — for the FIRST base. Edge +1.8 to +2.2R, holds train→test.
  • Bases 1–3, not just base 1 — Signal decays after base 1 but stays strongly tradeable through base 3 (~2.2–3.1R held-out), and pays MORE in 2023+. Later bases hold 62% of the capturable R in recent moves.
  • Structural swing-low stop — Beats the entry-day-wick stop on win rate AND control behaviour. The consistency lever (Phase 3) and your contract's 'structural stop'.
  • 3-bar shakeout hold — Free improvement everywhere — stops a normal pullback knocking you out in the first 3 days.
  • Free roll (trim ½ at +2R → BE) — Nearly halves yearly variance (0.49→0.29) for the same median return. The 'more consistent' you asked for.
  • 50-MA trail for the runner — Highest total R on real movers — lets the rare monster run.
  • Continuation (leg 2+) entries — Positive at every extension, better-behaved on control than base breakouts; ~3× the R captured per move.

✗ What doesn't (dropped / corrected)

  • Treating the signal as high-edge — It isn't. Control expectancy ≈0. The edge is survivorship; profit comes from risk control + the right tail.
  • The old 4-filter stack — −36% overfit tax; cir_gt75_gaps and tight_lt5 are noise that flip sign on test.
  • Regime / EMA-stack / near-high filters (as return edges) — Can't be judged on winners-only data — they're about avoiding losers, so they only matter against the control group.
  • Entry-day-wick stop (LOD) — More stop-outs, worse control behaviour. Your contract already says not to use it; the data agrees.
  • Volume-surge as a prerequisite — Only ~26% of winner breakouts were ≥2× volume. Useful as a weak tilt, not a gate.
  • Expecting the database win rates live — 50–60% on winners-only ≈ ~30% in the wild. Plan for that.

The locked strategy, in full

Universe — ADR 4–8%, ≥$5M/day dollar volume.
Entry — breakout (20/50-day high) or gap ≥5% on ≥1.5× volume, only if early (≤3 triggers off the 63-day low) and not extended (<50% above it).
Stop — structural swing low (10-bar low, capped 1.5×ATR). This defines 1R.
Hold — 3-bar shakeout guard: no trailing-MA exit in the first 3 closes.
Exit — Mode B (default, consistent) — trim ½ at +2R, move stop to breakeven, trail the rest on the 50-MA. ~60% win, ~half the yearly variance.
Exit — Mode A (high conviction) — no trim, hold to a close below the 50-MA. Highest total R.
Continuation (optional) — also enter on a pullback that reclaims the 20-EMA inside an uptrend, at any extension. Catches later legs and re-enters after a shakeout. Off by default.

Honest limits

  • The control group is built from ex-monster tickers, so it's trendier than a random universe — it understates the true false-positive rate. Directionally right, not a substitute for live forward testing.
  • Winners-only selection still inflates the absolute winner-side numbers; trust the relative rankings and the control column, not the headline win rates.
  • Daily bars only. The portfolio-level risk overlays from your contract (2.5% cap, heat caps, circuit breakers) are sizing rules layered on top — out of scope for the signal engine.
  • My backtest data ends ~Mar–Apr 2026, staler than your live TradingView charts, so a few of the explosive legs you've seen are partly beyond the tested data (but structurally identical to tested moves).

Glossary

Plain-English definitions — stays in view as you scroll.

How the test works

R — the risk unit
Everything is measured in R. R = entry price − stop price — what you lose if stopped out. A +3R trade made 3× what you risked; −1R is a full stop-out. R makes a $5 stock and a $500 stock directly comparable.
Train / test split
The 949 tickers are split 50/50 by symbol. The strategy is chosen on the train half, then measured untouched on the held-out test half. Survives test → it generalises. Collapses → it was overfit (fitted to noise).
Control group
Signals that fired on charts that did not become recorded big movers (any date outside a winning window, across 984 tickers). This is the false-positive population — what the same trigger does in the wild. Winners-only data can't show it.
Survivorship
The trap of studying only winners. A 60% win rate on winners-only data does NOT mean 60% live — the losers were never in the dataset. The control group is the antidote.

The numbers in the tables

avg R
Average R per trade in that bucket — the raw expectancy.
w99 R (capped)
Average R after capping the top 1% of winners (99th percentile). One 80R freak can flatter an average; this shows the edge without leaning on a single trade. We rank by this.
win
Win rate — % of trades that closed positive.
med R
The middle trade. Often negative here — the strategy wins through a minority of big trades, not by being right often.
R/30d
R earned per 30 days held — return per unit of time, so fast and slow strategies compare fairly.
edge
For a filter: w99-R of trades that PASS it minus those that FAIL it. Positive = the filter helps; the number is how much. Holds = same sign on train and test; flips = noise.
year std / worst year
Spread of yearly average-R (lower = steadier) and the single worst year. Consistency measures.

Entry filters (the codes)

Enter early ordinal_le3
The 'ordinal' is which numbered breakout this is, counting from the most recent 63-day low. ≤3 = one of the first three pushes off the base — early, not chasing a stock that already ran.
Not extended extension_lt50
How far price has stretched above its 63-day low, in %. <50% = still within 50% of the recent floor.
Volume ≥1.5× relvol_ge15
Today's volume vs the 20-day average. ≥1.5× = at least 50% heavier than normal (institutional participation).
Strong gap close cir_gt75_gaps
'Close-in-range' = where the close sits in the day's high–low bar. >75% = top quarter. Applied only to gap-up entries.
Tight base tight_lt5
Std-dev of the last 10 closes as a % of price. <5% = calm, coiled base.
Market uptrend spy_above_50
Is SPY above its 50-day average at the entry bar — a market-regime gauge.
EMA stack stack_bull
10-EMA > 20-EMA > 50-SMA with price on top — an aligned uptrend.
Near highs near_high_25
Price within 25% of its 52-week high.

Stop, exit & strategy terms

ATR
Average True Range — average daily price travel over 20 days. A volatility unit.
Structural / swing-low stop
Stop under the lowest low of the last 10 bars (capped at 1.5×ATR) — under the chart structure, not under today's candle wick.
3-bar hold
The trailing exit can't fire in the first 3 days after entry, so a normal shakeout doesn't knock you out early.
50-MA trail (E50)
Exit when price closes below its 50-period moving average — the default 'let it run' exit.
Free roll
At +2R, sell half and move the stop to breakeven. The remaining half is now 'free' (can't lose). Raises win rate and cuts variance.
Mode A / Mode B
A Runner = hold the whole position to the 50-MA break (max R). B Consistent = free roll (steadier). Same entry and stop.
Continuation / leg 2
A later entry: a pullback that reclaims the 20-EMA inside an established uptrend, taken after the move is already underway.
Extension band
Grouping entries by how far above the 63-day low they fired: 0–50%, 50–100%, etc.
